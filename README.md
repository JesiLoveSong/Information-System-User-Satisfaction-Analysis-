# Faculty Information System User Experience & Satisfaction Assessment

**Project Overview**
---

This project evaluated faculty experiences with the university's Faculty Information System and related review processes by analyzing post-workflow satisfaction surveys collected from faculty participants. I collected and analyzed quantitative and qualitative survey responses, identified recurring themes and user experience challenges, and developed executive reports to help leadership understand system usability, support effectiveness, and opportunities for process improvement.

**Question**
---

How satisfied are faculty with the Faculty Information System and associated review processes, and what improvements can be made to enhance the user experience?

**Project Objectives**
---

- Measure faculty satisfaction with key components of the Faculty Information System.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of system resources, training materials, and support services.
- Identify common usability challenges and process-related concerns.
- Analyze qualitative feedback to understand faculty experiences and improvement opportunities.
- Provide leadership with actionable recommendations to improve system adoption, support services, and process design.
- Support continuous improvement efforts through data-informed assessment.

**Data Sources**
---

- Faculty satisfaction Qualtrics surveys
- FIS utilization metric reports

**Methodology**
---

**Data Collection and Preparation**

- Collected survey responses from faculty participants following completion of faculty workflows.
- Compiled quantitative satisfaction metrics and qualitative feedback responses.
- Cleaned, categorized, and organized survey data for analysis.
  
**Data Analysis**

- Conducted descriptive analysis of user satisfaction, resource utilization, and support effectiveness.
- Examined patterns in system usability, support services, training resources, and process experiences.
- Performed thematic analysis of open-ended comments to identify recurring strengths, concerns, and recommendations.
- Compared satisfaction levels across different support resources and engagement activities.

**Reporting and Visualization**

- Created visualizations using Canva highlighting user experiences, resource effectiveness, and support outcomes.
- Presented findings to leadership and administrative stakeholders to support continuous improvement initiatives.

**Key Findings**
---

- Approximately 78% of respondents reported that the system was at least somewhat easy to use, suggesting generally positive usability perceptions while highlighting opportunities for improvement.
- Faculty support services received strong ratings, with approximately 93% of respondents reporting support resources as useful.
- Educational resources and guidance materials were also well received, with approximately 93% of respondents finding support documentation useful.
- Faculty workshops and help sessions demonstrated particularly high satisfaction levels, with approximately 94% of respondents rating sessions as useful and over 90% indicating that their questions were adequately addressed.
- Approximately 80% of respondents reported satisfaction with the resolution of technical issues.

**Qualitative feedback identified several recurring strengths, including:**

- Responsive and helpful support staff
- Valuable training and informational sessions
- Useful documentation and resources
- 
**Common improvement opportunities included:**
- Greater clarity regarding expectations and required materials
- Improved document upload and file management processes
- More intuitive system navigation and interface design
- Clearer communication regarding workflow timelines and milestones

**Business Recommendations**
---

- Develop more detailed instructions, examples, and workflow guides to address recurring requests for greater clarity regarding system processes and documentation requirements.
- Maintain support resources and help sessions, as survey responses consistently identified these services as one of the strongest aspects of the user experience.
- Provide earlier and more frequent communication regarding expectations, deadlines, workflow milestones, and available support resources.
- Implement recurring assessment and feedback mechanisms to monitor faculty experiences and identify emerging concerns before they become widespread issues.

**Analytical Limitations**
---

- Survey results reflect participant perceptions and self-reported experiences rather than objective measures of system performance.
- Findings reflect feedback collected following specific faculty review workflows and may not represent the experiences of all system users.
- Qualitative themes represent the most commonly reported feedback and may not capture the full range of user experiences across the institution.
- Results should be interpreted as **a snapshot of some user perceptions at a specific point** in time rather than a comprehensive evaluation of long-term user satisfaction.

**Conclusion**
---

The assessment demonstrated generally positive faculty perceptions of the Faculty Information System's support resources, training materials, and user assistance services. Faculty particularly valued support staff responsiveness, educational resources, and workflow guidance, resulting in high satisfaction levels across multiple support-related measures.

At the same time, the analysis identified opportunities to improve user experience through clearer communication, enhanced documentation, more intuitive navigation, and streamlined document management processes. By combining quantitative satisfaction metrics with qualitative feedback, the project provided leadership with actionable insights to support continuous improvement, strengthen faculty engagement, and enhance the overall effectiveness of the Faculty Information System.
